Measuring GEO: How B2B Manufacturers Track AI Citations

You optimized for AI search. Now how do you know it worked? A practical measurement framework — the four signals that actually show AI visibility, a weekly prompt tracker, how to read AI crawler logs, and a 90-day benchmark plan built for factories, not SaaS companies.

Most "GEO is the new SEO" articles stop at tactics — llms.txt, schema, FAQ pages. Few tell you how to prove any of it moved the needle. For a manufacturer with a static site and a small team, that proof is the whole point: you need to know whether the hours spent on content and structured data are earning citations, or just burning time. This guide is the measurement half of the playbook.

1. Why measuring GEO is not measuring SEO

SEO gives you a clean, positional number: rank 3 for "stainless steel bottle manufacturer." GEO does not. An AI answer is non-deterministic — ask the same prompt twice and you may get two different answers, with or without your brand. Citation is also binary: you are either named/sourced or you are not; there is no "position 3." That means GEO is measured as presence and share-of-voice across many prompts over time, not as one ranking you can screenshot on Monday.

3. Build a weekly prompt tracker

This is the core instrument and it costs nothing. Make a simple sheet with two prompt families:

  • Branded prompts — "Who makes SSMug drinkware?", "SSMug vs [competitor]", "Is SSMug a factory or trading company?". These confirm your own name is surfacing.
  • Category prompts — "best insulated water bottle manufacturer in China", "OEM stainless steel tumbler supplier with LFGB", "private label drinkware low MOQ". These are where new buyers discover you.

Run 10–20 prompts across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Claude once a week. Log: did the engine cite you (Y/N), the exact phrase used, and whether a link or source appeared. Over 90 days a pattern emerges that a single check never shows.

4. Reading AI crawler & referral logs

AI engines send identifiable bots to read your site. In your server logs or a log analyser, watch for: GPTBot (ChatGPT), PerplexityBot, Google-Extended (Gemini / AI Overviews), and ClaudeBot. Frequent, successful (200) crawls of your blog and product pages are a leading indicator — they cannot cite what they have not read.

In GA4, segment sessions by source. AI referral often appears as chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai, or as "direct" spikes with no associated keyword and a fast bounce-back pattern. Do not dismiss these as bots only — some carry real users who clicked a cited source.

Realistic expectation: GEO is a compounding, multi-market gain, not a ranking jump. SSMug ships to 50+ countries, so a citation win in one language or region rarely moves total traffic hard in a month — but across a year it is the difference between being a sourced supplier and an invisible one.

6. Tools: manual, free and paid

  • Manual (free): a spreadsheet prompt tracker + GA4 + server logs. Enough for most factories.
  • Free-ish: Bing Webmaster / Google Search Console for crawl stats; your host's log viewer for bot evidence.
  • Paid: GEO-specific platforms (Peec, Otterly, Goodie) that automate prompt monitoring across engines and alert on citation changes. Worth it once you have 20+ prompts and a real content program.

7. Common measurement mistakes

  • Judging after two weeks. Citation is noisy; 90 days minimum.
  • Tracking one prompt. You need a set, scored for share-of-voice.
  • Counting crawls as wins. Crawling is necessary, not sufficient — citation is the proof.
  • Ignoring llms.txt / sitemap. If the engines cannot parse your site, no signal will move. Keep both current (we maintain ours openly at ssmug.com/llms.txt).
  • Optimizing for the wrong engine. Perplexity and Gemini reward source links; ChatGPT rewards well-structured, frequently-cited facts. Track each separately.

8. FAQ

How is measuring GEO different from measuring SEO?
SEO is measured by rankings on a search-results page — a stable, positional metric. GEO is measured by whether an AI answer cites or mentions you, which is non-deterministic: the same prompt can return different answers, and citation is binary (cited or not) rather than a rank. You measure presence and share-of-voice across many prompts over time, not a single number.

Which AI engines should a B2B manufacturer track?
Start with the four that matter for B2B discovery: ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google's Gemini (and AI Overviews), and Claude. Perplexity and Gemini are the most citation- and source-link transparent; ChatGPT and Claude are best tracked via branded and category prompts plus crawler-log evidence.

Can I see AI referral traffic in Google Analytics?
Yes, but it is easy to miss. AI engines visit with identifiable bots (GPTBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended, ClaudeBot) and some send referral hits to your server. In GA4, segment by source/medium and look for 'chatgpt.com', 'perplexity.ai', 'gemini' or direct spikes with no landing-page keyword. Server logs give the cleanest bot-level view.

How long before GEO effort shows a measurable result?
Treat 90 days as the minimum window. AI engines refresh their retrieval and model behavior slowly, and citation is noisy week to week. Track a fixed prompt set weekly and judge trend at the 90-day mark, not after two weeks. SSMug ships to 50+ countries, so citation gains compound across many small markets rather than one.
