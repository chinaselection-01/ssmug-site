A manufacturer that sells into 50+ countries from one English site is leaving the majority of its addressable buyers to a competitor who bothered to speak their language. But multilingual is where good B2B sites go to die: split authority, broken hreflang, and machine-translated pages that read like a warning label. This report lays out the architecture and signals that keep a multilingual factory site both rankable and citable.

1. Why multilingual matters for B2B factories

Two forces make this urgent in 2026. First, buyer journeys now start with a local-language query — a German procurement manager searches "isolierte Trinkflaschen Hersteller", not "insulated bottle manufacturer China". A monolingual site is simply invisible to that query. Second, AI answer engines now respond in the user's language and cite the clearest local-language source. If your only presence is an English page, you are cited less — or not at all — in non-English markets.

The good news for factories: you do not need 30 languages. A focused set — English as the hub, plus the languages of your top three importing regions (for us, German, Polish and Arabic alongside English) — captures the overwhelming majority of inbound intent. Depth in a few locales beats thin coverage of many.

2. Architecture: ccTLD vs subfolder vs subdomain

Where you put the localized pages decides how much authority they inherit and how much you maintain. The table contrasts the three standard options:

Subdomainde.ssmug.comPartially separateMediumWhen content/team is regionally split

For a factory on a static GitHub Pages setup, subfolders are the obvious winner: one deploy, one sitemap mindset, and all link equity flows to the same root. Reserve a ccTLD only if you have a local entity and the budget to earn authority from zero in each market.

3. hreflang done right

hreflang tells search engines which page serves which language and region, so the right version shows to the right user. The rules that trip up factories:

  • Reciprocity: every localized page must reference every other language version, including itself (x-default pointing to the English hub is sensible).
  • Consistent URLs: the German page links back to the exact Polish and English URLs, with no trailing-slash drift.
  • One return link only: duplicate or mismatched tags trigger the famous "no return tags" warning and can suppress both pages.
  • Language + region where relevant: de-de (German, Germany) vs de-at (German, Austria) when you genuinely localize pricing or compliance.

4. Translation vs transcreation

Machine translation is acceptable for dense spec tables; it is dangerous on the pages where trust is won. Three things must be human-reviewed in every locale:

  • Certification names: FDA (US), LFGB (Germany), and the local phrasing buyers search for. A mistranslated compliance claim can read as a false one.
  • Units and standards: metric vs imperial, and region-specific references (e.g. "BPA-free" vs local equivalents).
  • Trade and category terms: "tumbler", "flask", "growler" and "thermos" map inconsistently across languages; use the term local buyers actually type.

Transcreation — rewriting the message for the market rather than word-swapping — pays off most on category and landing pages, where a buyer decides whether you are a real factory or a dropshipping shell.

5. Schema & llms.txt per locale

Structured data is language-agnostic in mechanism but locale-aware in content. For each localized page:

  • Add inLanguage to the page's schema so crawlers and AI models know the language.
  • Use Organization with the same @id across locales, and sameAs to your profiles, so the entity stays unified globally.
  • Include Product / Offer with locale-correct currency and availability.
  • In llms.txt, list canonical locale pages and note each language, or serve a locale-aware llms.txt so an AI system can select the right-language source for a query.
Rule of thumb: keep one canonical entity identity (the factory) across all languages, but let each locale's facts — currency, certifications, contact — be genuinely local. Unifying the entity builds global authority; localizing the facts earns local citations.

6. Getting cited by AI in multiple languages

AI engines cite the clearest, most authoritative answer in the user's language. To be that answer:

  • Write for the question, in the language: each locale page should directly answer a local query ("hersteller edelstahl trinkflasche privätlabel") with a concise, factual block near the top.
  • Keep facts consistent across languages: the factory's founding year (2017), capacity (500,000+ units/month), model count (167+) and certifications (SGS, TÜV, ISO 9001, FDA, LFGB) must match everywhere — conflicting numbers erode entity trust.
  • Use comparison tables and FAQ schema per locale; these are the snippets AI pulls most often.
  • Earn local links (trade directories, local trade-show pages) rather than relying on the English backlink profile to carry each language.

8. FAQ

Which URL structure is best for a multilingual manufacturer site?
For most factories, a subfolder structure (example.com/de/) is the lowest-risk choice: it inherits existing domain authority, is simple to host, and stays consistent with hreflang. A ccTLD maximizes local trust but splits authority and multiplies maintenance. A subdomain sits in between and is harder to associate with the root.

Is machine translation good enough for B2B pages?
For technical spec tables it is a start, but not enough where purchase intent lives. Product names, certifications (FDA, LFGB), units and trade terms must be native-reviewed. Transcreation matters most on category and landing pages where buyers judge whether you are a credible factory.

Do AI search engines read non-English pages?
Yes. ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini surface answers in the user's language and cite clearly written, well-structured, locally relevant sources in that language. A clean, hreflang-tagged, schema-rich localized page beats a weak machine-translated one.

Should I put each language in llms.txt?
If you keep a single llms.txt, list the canonical locale pages and note each language so a crawler understands the set. More practically, serve a locale-aware llms.txt or include per-page structured data (inLanguage) so AI systems select the right-language source.
