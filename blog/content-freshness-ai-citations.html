Content Freshness: How Often Should a Manufacturer Update Pages?

Ask ChatGPT or Perplexity for a drinkware manufacturer and the answer will often include a capacity figure, a certification list, a port of shipment or an MOQ range. Those facts age. A factory that added three models, changed its sample lead time or renewed a certificate in January but left its website untouched since the previous year is not just stale — it is a poor source to cite. Freshness is one of the quieter inputs into whether an AI engine treats a page as reliable, and unlike backlinks it is entirely under your control.

1. Why freshness matters in an AI answer

Answer engines assemble responses from sources they judge usable. A page that is detailed but clearly out of date creates a risk: if the engine repeats a number that has since changed, the answer looks wrong and the source looks careless. Pages that visibly reflect the current state of a business are safer to quote, especially for the operational facts buyers ask about — capacity, lead time, MOQ, certifications and available models.

For a manufacturer this is an advantage. Most factories publish a brochure once and never touch it. A site with a consistent, documented update rhythm gives both buyers and machines a reason to come back. The goal is not to publish more pages; it is to keep the pages you already have accurate.

2. What "fresh" actually means

Freshness is not the same as recency of publication. A guide written three years ago that still describes the process correctly is a good source; a page published last week with generic filler is not. Three things signal real freshness:

  • Factual accuracy. The numbers on the page match the current operation — today's model list, today's certification scope, today's realistic lead-time range.
  • Visible revision. A dated change that a reader can see: new examples, an updated table, a clarified answer to a question buyers keep asking.
  • Consistency across surfaces. The website, llms.txt, sitemap and any structured data tell the same story. A product page saying one capacity and a machine-readable file saying another undermines both.

Treat a last-updated date as a claim you can defend. If a visitor checks the page against your current catalog and finds a model you stopped making two years ago, the date has cost you trust.

3. Four page types to refresh first

Not every page deserves the same attention. Prioritise the ones that carry facts a buyer might quote or act on:

Page typeWhat changesSuggested trigger
Product & specification pagesModels, capacities, finishes, lids, accessoriesAny catalog or spec change
MOQ, pricing & lead-time guidesTypical ranges, sample timing, packing termsQuarterly review or process change
Compliance & certification pagesCertificate scope, test standards, document setsRenewal or new market entry
Comparison & "how to choose" guidesExamples, tables, answers to new buyer questionsEvery 60–90 days

Contact details, company name and address belong in this list too. An AI answer that quotes an outdated email address is worse than no citation at all.

4. A repeatable refresh workflow

Freshness fails when it depends on someone remembering. Give it a place in the calendar and a short checklist. The table below is a practical starting point, not an industry rule — adjust the cadence to how often your own facts change.

StepActionOutput
1. Collect changesList what changed since the last review: models, specs, certificates, lead times, policiesOne change log
2. Map pagesMatch each change to every page and machine-readable file that mentions itA page-by-page task list
3. Edit factsUpdate tables, examples and FAQ answers; keep indicative figures labelled as indicativeRevised pages
4. Update datesSet a visible last-updated line and the structured dateModified field to the same real dateConsistent date signals
5. Sync surfacesRefresh llms.txt descriptions and check sitemap entries still resolveMatching external signals
6. Re-checkAsk a few real buyer questions in an AI engine and note whether the answer still reflects your siteA short citation log
Keep it honest: never update a date without updating the content. A page that claims a recent revision but still quotes an old model list is a trust failure with a timestamp attached — and buyers notice it faster than a machine does.

5. Three freshness signals to show

Do not rely on a single hidden field. Show freshness in ways both readers and crawlers can use:

  • A visible last-updated line near the top or bottom of the article, in plain language.
  • Structured metadata — datePublished and dateModified in the page's JSON-LD, matching the visible date.
  • A maintained llms.txt and sitemap whose descriptions reflect the current version of each guide, so machine-readable sources agree with the page itself.

These three should tell one story. If the visible date says November but the structured data still says the previous year, you have introduced a contradiction where there was no need for one.

6. The mistakes that break trust

Four patterns show up again and again on manufacturer sites:

  • Date bumping. Changing the date, the font and nothing else. It looks like manipulation once someone compares versions.
  • Silent spec drift. Production changes but the website does not. The page keeps getting cited for numbers that are no longer true.
  • Unlabelled estimates. Writing a price or MOQ as a fixed figure when it is only an indicative range. Buyers arrive with the wrong expectation and leave.
  • Orphan updates. Refreshing a page but not the llms.txt line, sitemap or FAQ that repeats the same fact. Machines see two versions of the company.

The fix is unglamorous: keep a change log, review on a schedule, and label what is stable versus what is confirmed per inquiry. That is what makes a page safe to quote.

7. FAQ

How often should a B2B manufacturer update website content?
There is no universal rule. A practical starting point is a full audit every 60 to 90 days, product and specification pages checked whenever capacity, certifications, models or lead times change, and evergreen guides reviewed less often but kept factually current.

Does changing the date on a page improve AI citations?
Changing a date without changing the content adds nothing and can damage trust. Treat the date as a record of a real revision: update the figures and examples first, then set the visible date and the structured dateModified field to the same day.

Which pages should be refreshed first?
Product and specification pages, MOQ and lead-time guides, compliance pages, and comparison or "how to choose" guides. These carry the facts buyers act on and the facts most often quoted in an AI answer.

What facts should be kept current on a product page?
Model names, capacities, materials, finishes, lids, certification references, MOQ and lead-time ranges, port of shipment and contact details. Be specific about what is stable and honest about what is confirmed per inquiry.
