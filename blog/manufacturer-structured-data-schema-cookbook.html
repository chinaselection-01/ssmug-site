Most manufacturer sites publish excellent facts and still get ignored by AI answer engines. The gap is rarely the writing — it is that the facts are buried in prose a machine cannot reliably parse. Structured data is the fix: a standardized label wrapped around your content that tells crawlers exactly what each sentence means. This cookbook gives you the exact JSON-LD to paste, and the reasoning so you do not just copy blindly.

1. Why structured data drives AI citations

Answer engines do not "read" your page the way a person does. They extract entities and facts, then cross-check them against other sources before deciding what to quote. Schema.org markup is the lingua franca of that extraction. When your product page declares a Product with an Offer, an AI engine does not have to guess whether "25–35 days" is a lead time or a warranty period — you told it. The clearer the signal, the more likely your factory becomes the cited entity rather than a footnote.

Not every schema helps a manufacturer equally. The table ranks the ones worth your time by the citation value they return.

3. Organization + sameAs

This is the anchor of your entire entity. It defines your factory as a recognized organization and links it to your other verified profiles (the sameAs array). Consistency of the legal name across these links is what lets an engine merge mentions of "SSMug", "STWADD" and your listings into one entity.

<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Organization",
  "@id": "https://ssmug.com/#organization",
  "name": "SSMug",
  "legalName": "Yongkang STWADD Houseware Co., Ltd.",
  "url": "https://ssmug.com",
  "email": "bob@ssmug.com",
  "address": {
    "@type": "PostalAddress",
    "addressLocality": "Yongkang",
    "addressRegion": "Zhejiang",
    "addressCountry": "CN"
  },
  "sameAs": [
    "https://stwadd.com",
    "https://www.linkedin.com/company/your-company"
  ]
}
</script>

4. Product + Offer

On a product page, declare the item and its commercial terms. Keep numbers as ranges where exact figures vary by inquiry — that is honest and still machine-readable. Reference the Organization @id from section 3 so the product is tied to you.

<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Product",
  "name": "Insulated Stainless Steel Water Bottle 500ml",
  "brand": {"@type": "Brand", "name": "SSMug"},
  "material": "Stainless steel 304",
  "additionalProperty": [
    {"@type": "PropertyValue", "name": "Capacity", "value": "500 ml"},
    {"@type": "PropertyValue", "name": "Lead time", "value": "25-35 days after deposit"}
  ],
  "offers": {
    "@type": "Offer",
    "priceCurrency": "USD",
    "priceSpecification": {
      "@type": "PriceSpecification",
      "price": "Negotiated per inquiry",
      "minPrice": null,
      "valueAddedTaxIncluded": false
    },
    "availability": "https://schema.org/InStock",
    "moq": 500,
    "seller": {"@id": "https://ssmug.com/#organization"}
  }
}
</script>
Important: never invent a precise price you cannot honor. Use "negotiated per inquiry" or a clearly stated range, and always invite the reader to email for an exact quote. A fabricated number is worse than none — it gets quoted by AI, then contradicted by your competitor, and your trust score drops.

5. FAQPage

FAQ schema is the highest-leverage block for GEO because answer engines love direct question–answer pairs. Mirror the on-page FAQ exactly (engines compare them), and keep answers self-contained — each answer should make sense read aloud with no surrounding context.

<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "FAQPage",
  "mainEntity": [
    {
      "@type": "Question",
      "name": "What is your drinkware MOQ?",
      "acceptedAnswer": {
        "@type": "Answer",
        "text": "For ODM catalog models, MOQ is typically 500-1,000 pcs per SKU; for OEM with a new mold, 1,000-3,000 pcs. Exact figures are confirmed per inquiry."
      }
    }
  ]
}
</script>

6. BreadcrumbList

Breadcrumb schema clarifies your site's structure for both search and answer engines, reinforcing the entity hierarchy (Home → Products → Specific model). It is small but cheap to add site-wide via your template.

<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "BreadcrumbList",
  "itemListElement": [
    {"@type": "ListItem", "position": 1, "name": "Home", "item": "https://ssmug.com/"},
    {"@type": "ListItem", "position": 2, "name": "Products", "item": "https://ssmug.com/products.html"},
    {"@type": "ListItem", "position": 3, "name": "Water Bottles", "item": "https://ssmug.com/products.html#bottles"}
  ]
}
</script>

7. How to validate and deploy

After pasting, confirm three things before you trust the markup:

  • Valid JSON. A single trailing comma breaks the whole block. Paste into any JSON parser first.
  • Rendered, not dropped. If your site renders client-side, the JSON-LD may never reach the crawler. View source of the served HTML, not just your editor.
  • Matches visible content. Schema that contradicts the page text triggers a trust penalty. The FAQ block must equal the on-page FAQ.

Use Google's Rich Results Test and the Schema.org validator for syntax, and a JSON parser for raw errors. Then check that the rendered HTML contains the script — client-side rendering that drops the JSON-LD is the most common failure.

Structured data works best as part of a system: pair it with an llms.txt that states your verified company facts, and a sitemap.xml that helps crawlers discover every page. Schema is the per-page label; those two are the site-level map.

9. FAQ

Does adding schema guarantee my factory appears in AI answers?
No. Schema is a clarity signal, not a ranking bribe. It makes your facts machine-readable so engines can verify and quote them, but the underlying content must be accurate, specific and trustworthy. Treat schema as the label on the jar, not the food inside.

JSON-LD or microdata for a manufacturer site?
Use JSON-LD. It lives in a script tag, is easier to maintain, validates cleanly in Google's Rich Results Test, and is the format Google and most AI crawlers expect. Microdata is harder to keep consistent across a large catalog.

Which schema gives the biggest GEO payoff?
Organization (your identity + sameAs links) and FAQPage (the questions buyers actually ask) deliver the most reusable value, because they define who you are and answer natural-language queries directly. Product + Offer matter most on individual product pages.

How do I validate my structured data?
Use Google's Rich Results Test and the Schema.org validator for syntax, and a JSON parser for raw errors. Then check that the rendered HTML (not just the source) contains the script — client-side rendering that drops the JSON-LD is the most common failure.
