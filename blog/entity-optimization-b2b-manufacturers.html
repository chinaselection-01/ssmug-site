Entity Optimization for B2B Manufacturers

Pages get indexed; entities get cited. If you want ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini to name your factory when a buyer asks "who makes insulated drinkware in China," you need to exist as a recognized entity — not just as a page that once ranked. Here is the operating manual.

Most manufacturer SEO advice stops at "rank for keywords." That was the right goal in 2015. In 2026, a growing share of B2B discovery happens inside answer engines that do not show a list of ten blue links — they synthesize a response and name the suppliers they trust. Those engines reason about entities: stable, well-described things in a knowledge graph. This guide explains how a factory becomes one.

1. What "entity" means for a factory

An entity is a thing a machine can recognize as distinct and describe with attributes: a company, a product line, a certification, a location. Your factory is an entity — Yongkang STWADD Houseware Co., Ltd. — and so is the brand on top of it, SSMug. A blog post is not an entity; it is evidence about one.

The practical difference: a keyword-optimized page can lose rank overnight and vanish from discovery. An entity, once established with consistent facts across the web, persists across engines and queries. When a buyer asks an AI "who is a reliable insulated bottle OEM in Yongkang," the engine matches the query to the entity it has seen described consistently — not to a single URL.

2. Why AI engines cite entities, not pages

Answer engines retrieve passages, then decide which sources are credible enough to quote. Credibility is assessed at the entity level: is this company one the model has seen described reliably elsewhere? If your facts contradict each other across pages — "founded 2017" on one, "since 2010" on another; "ISO 9001" claimed but absent from your structured data — the model downgrades confidence and cites a competitor with cleaner signals.

This is why the classic playbook (publish more pages, build more links) is necessary but not sufficient. You also need the entity behind those pages to be unambiguous and corroborated.

3. The four entity signals that matter

Four signals do most of the work. Get them right and the rest is reinforcement:

Key idea: your llms.txt is the entity manifest. Treat it as the single source of truth that AI engines should read first — every fact there must match the structured data and the visible site, or you create the very contradiction that suppresses citations.

4. Building a consistent identity

"NAP" (Name–Address–Phone) consistency is local-SEO gospel; for B2B manufacturers the equivalent is LEC: Legal name, Entity attributes, Claims. Run a sheet with one row per fact and one column per surface (home, about, products, blog, llms.txt, directory profiles). Flag every mismatch. The facts worth locking:

  • Legal entity: Yongkang STWADD Houseware Co., Ltd. (永康市圣瓦达日用品有限公司)
  • Founded: 2017
  • Location: Yongkang, Zhejiang, China — Jinhua–Yongkang drinkware cluster
  • Certifications: SGS, TÜV, ISO 9001, FDA, LFGB, BPA Free
  • Capacity & catalog: 500,000+ units/month; 167+ models
  • Business model: manufacturer (factory-backed), not a trading company

5. Your Factory page as the entity hub

The /about.html (Factory) page should be the canonical description of the entity. Make it answer, in plain prose a model can parse: who you are, where you are, what you make, what you certify, and proof you are a factory (floor photos, equipment, test reports). Link from it to products, certifications and contact. Everything else on the site should point back. The hub-and-spoke shape is what an entity graph expects.

6. Structured data: Organization + sameAs

Add an Organization schema block to the site template (header or footer injection) with the locked facts, and use sameAs to point at your verified external profiles. This is the machine-readable handshake that connects your pages to the broader graph:

7. Earned citations: press, directories, proof

Self-published facts are necessary but not sufficient — a model wants outside confirmation. Highest-value, lowest-cost corroboration for a manufacturer:

  • Trade-show listings (HOMELIFE, Canton, regional expos) — third-party pages naming you as an exhibitor.
  • Supplier directories with consistent company facts and a verified badge.
  • Certification body pages where SGS/TÜV/ISO list your certificate number.
  • Buyer references / case notes that name markets served (50+ countries) without exposing private data.

Each external mention that repeats the same locked facts is a vote for the entity. Inconsistent mentions are votes against it.

8. A 60-day entity-building plan

Entity work is a short project, then maintenance:

  • Days 1–7: build the LEC fact sheet; audit all pages for contradictions; fix them.
  • Days 8–14: publish/finalize llms.txt; add Organization schema with sameAs; verify the Factory page is the hub.
  • Days 15–45: publish 2 citable articles/week (this is the cadence SSMug runs); pursue 3–5 external corroborations (directory, trade-show, cert page).
  • Days 46–60: re-run the contradiction audit; track AI citations via prompt testing; double down on the cluster that earns mentions.

9. FAQ

What is an "entity" in SEO and GEO?
An entity is a thing — a company, person, product or place — that search engines and AI models recognize as a distinct node with its own attributes. A page can rank or not; an entity persists across pages, queries and engines. For a factory, the entity is your legal company (Yongkang STWADD Houseware Co., Ltd.) and the brand built on it (SSMug).

How do I make my factory a recognized entity?
Make the same facts — legal name, founding year, location, certifications, product range — consistent everywhere they appear (site, llms.txt, directories, profiles), mark the company up with Organization schema and sameAs links, and earn third-party citations (trade-show listings, press, supplier directories) that confirm those facts from outside your domain.

Does social media help AI citation?
Indirectly. AI engines do not usually crawl every social post, but a consistent brand name and profile across LinkedIn, industry directories and trade platforms creates corroborating signals that strengthen the entity. The highest-leverage move is structured, crawlable facts on your own domain plus llms.txt.

How long until entity optimization pays off?
Foundational fixes (consistent facts, Organization schema, llms.txt) can be live in days. Earning steady AI citations typically takes 1–3 months of consistent publishing plus third-party corroboration, which is why a 60-day plan with weekly checkpoints beats a one-off cleanup.
