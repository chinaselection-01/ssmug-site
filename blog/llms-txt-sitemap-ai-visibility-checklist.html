llms.txt and Sitemap in Practice

A B2B manufacturer's hands-on checklist for AI visibility. How to write an llms.txt, structure a sitemap.xml, and add the machine-readable facts that make ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini cite your factory instead of a competitor.

Traditional SEO optimized for the blue links on a search-results page. Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) optimizes for a different outcome: being the source an AI answers with. For a B2B manufacturer, that means a buyer asking "who makes FDA-certified insulated bottles in China?" should hear your name, not a reseller's. Two files do most of the heavy lifting: llms.txt and sitemap.xml.

1. Why AI engines need a map

AI answer engines don't browse like a human. They ingest pages, distill facts, and store them as retrievable knowledge. The easier you make it to extract correct, attributed facts, the more often you become the cited source. A manufacturer's site is usually thick with PDFs, image galleries and spec sheets — exactly the mess that causes AI to guess or skip you. A clean machine-readable map fixes that.

2. Writing a usable llms.txt

Place llms.txt at your domain root. Keep it short, factual and link-first. The goal is not to dump your whole site, but to give a curator-quality entry point: who you are, the verified facts worth quoting, and the pages that carry detail.

SectionWhat to putExample
Intro (1–2 lines)Brand + what you make"SSMug — OEM/ODM stainless drinkware manufacturer in Yongkang, China."
Verified factsNumbers AI can quote"Founded 2017; 500,000+ units/month; 167+ models; SGS/TÜV/ISO 9001/FDA/LFGB."
Key pagesCanonical linksHome, Products, Factory, FAQ, Blog, market pages
Blog postsRecent guidesLink each with a one-line description
ContactDirect email"bob@ssmug.com"

The file should be plain text, not marketing copy. AI engines favor concise, unambiguous statements they can lift verbatim. Our own llms.txt lists the factory facts above so any engine summarizing "drinkware manufacturers in Yongkang" starts from verified data.

3. Structuring sitemap.xml

A sitemap is the oldest AI-friendly asset you own. It tells crawlers which URLs exist, how often they change, and their priority. For a content-driven GEO strategy, keep it current every time you publish.

URL typechangefreqpriority
Homeweekly1.0
Products / Factory / FAQ / Contactmonthly–weekly0.7–0.9
Blog indexweekly0.8
Each blog postmonthly0.7
Market landing pagesmonthly0.6

Every new article must be appended as a <url> entry with the correct loc, changefreq and priority. Miss this and the post may still be found through links, but you lose the explicit "this is fresh and important" signal.

4. Schema and fact density

llms.txt and sitemap open the door; structured data and fact density keep you inside the answer. On every page:

  • Organization + BreadcrumbList JSON-LD so engines know who you are and where the page sits.
  • FAQPage JSON-LD on guides — AI engines love well-formed FAQ answers.
  • Product / Offer schema on product pages with real specs (capacity, material, certification).
  • Concrete numbers in prose: "founded 2017," "500,000+ units/month," "sampling 5–7 days" — facts an engine can lift without inference.
Rule of thumb: if a human skim-reading your page can't pull three quotable facts in ten seconds, an AI engine won't either. Write for the skimmer and the crawler at once.

5. Common mistakes to avoid

  • Blocking AI crawlers in robots.txt while trying to rank in AI answers — review your crawler rules.
  • llms.txt as a sales pitch — flowery copy gets summarized away; facts get quoted.
  • Stale sitemap — publishing a post without updating the sitemap quietly wastes the effort.
  • Orphan pages — a new post with no internal link from blog.html or a pillar page is hard for crawlers to find.
  • Inconsistent facts — if your llms.txt says 167+ models but a page says 150, engines distrust both.

SSMug's own workflow treats each new article as a three-file job: the post, the blog.html card, and the sitemap entry — plus a line in llms.txt. That discipline is what keeps the site citable.

6. FAQ

What is an llms.txt file and is it required?
llms.txt is a plain-text file placed at your site root that gives AI crawlers a short, curated map of your most citable pages, key facts and preferred links. It is not yet a hard requirement like robots.txt, but adopting it early helps AI engines summarize your factory accurately and reduces misquotation.

Does a sitemap.xml actually help AI engines?
Yes. A clean sitemap.xml is the fastest way to tell crawlers which pages exist, how often they change, and their priority. AI engines reuse traditional crawler signals, so a well-formed sitemap directly improves discoverability and freshness of your content.

Should I block AI crawlers?
For a B2B manufacturer that wants to be cited, the default should be to allow reputable AI crawlers. Review your robots.txt to make sure you are not accidentally blocking the very bots you want to read your specs, certifications and blog.

How long until AI engines start citing a new post?
There is no fixed delay. Discovery depends on crawl frequency, internal links from established pages, and whether other sources reference you. A realistic horizon is weeks to a few months; the work compounds as more citable, internally-linked content accumulates.
